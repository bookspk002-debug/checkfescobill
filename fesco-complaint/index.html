FESCO Complaint — How to File Online (November 2026)

Wrong bill, bill never arrived, faulty meter, voltage problem or a delayed new connection — here are the three ways to complain to FESCO, what to keep ready, and how to follow up until it is fixed.

Updated: November 2026

Three ways to complain

FESCO serves around 4 to 5.4 million consumers across eight districts, so complaints are handled through three official channels. Use whichever is easiest for you; all three reach the same complaint system.

  1. Call the helpline — 118 (toll-free). The fastest option for most people. Call 118, explain the problem, and give your 14-digit reference number. The operator registers the complaint and gives you a complaint number — keep it safe.
  2. Lodge it online — CCMS portal. The Customer Complaint Management System (CCMS) is at ccms.pitc.com.pk. You can lodge a complaint there and track its status later using your complaint number.
  3. Visit your subdivision office. Go to your nearest FESCO subdivision office in person. This is the right choice for problems that need documents or a meter inspection, and for escalation when a complaint has not been resolved. Take your CNIC and a copy of your bill.

What to keep ready before you complain

Having these ready makes the complaint faster:

  • Your 14-digit reference number — printed at the top-left of any old bill. Almost every complaint about a bill or a meter needs this number.
  • Your CNIC — the complaint is registered against the account holder.
  • A copy of the bill — the bill you are complaining about, or your most recent bill if the problem is that no bill arrived.
  • A photo of your meter reading — essential if you believe the bill is wrong. Photograph the meter display clearly, showing the reading, and note the date and time.
  • Your complaint number — once you have lodged the complaint, write it down and keep it with the bill.

Before complaining about a wrong bill, check the meter yourself. Compare the "present reading" printed on your bill with the reading on your physical meter. If the bill reading is higher than the meter shows, the bill is wrong and a re-reading should fix it. If the readings match, the high bill is more likely high usage or a slab change — check the unit rate page before you complain.

Complaint types — and what happens for each

Wrong bill / wrong meter reading

The most common complaint. Ask for a re-reading of your meter. FESCO sends a reader to check the physical meter against the reading on the bill. If the bill reading is higher than the actual reading, the bill is corrected and a revised bill is issued. If the reading is correct, the bill stands.

Bill not received

If your paper bill never arrives, do not wait — the due date still applies and a late payment surcharge can follow. Check your bill immediately on the official PITC portal at bill.pitc.com.pk/fescobill with your 14-digit reference number, or text it to 8118 for the amount and due date, and download a duplicate bill to pay from. Then complain so the delivery problem is recorded.

Voltage problem

Low voltage, voltage fluctuation or frequent tripping can damage appliances. Report it on 118 or CCMS with your reference number and describe the problem — for example, whether it happens at a particular time of day or affects the whole street.

Faulty meter

If your meter is not running, is running too fast, shows a blank display or looks damaged, report it as a faulty meter. Do not open or tamper with the meter yourself — that can be treated as meter tampering. FESCO will inspect the meter and, if it is faulty, replace it.

New connection delay

If you applied for a new connection and it is delayed beyond what you were told, complain with your application or demand-notice details. New connections are handled through the ENC system at fesco.enc.com.pk, and the complaint should quote your application reference so it can be traced.

Billing correction — step by step

  1. Check your meter. Compare the present reading on the bill with your physical meter and photograph the meter.
  2. Lodge the complaint. Call 118 or use the CCMS portal at ccms.pitc.com.pk, asking for a re-reading and a billing correction.
  3. Save the complaint number. Write it down with the date you complained.
  4. A re-reading is done. A meter reader visits and records the actual reading.
  5. A corrected bill is issued. If the reading was wrong, a revised bill is issued with the corrected units. Check the new bill line by line.
  6. Pay the corrected bill by its due date. If you already paid the wrong bill, ask at the subdivision office how the excess will be adjusted.

If your complaint is not resolved — escalation

If the complaint sits unresolved, follow up on the CCMS portal or by calling 118, quoting your complaint number each time, and note the date of every follow-up. If it is still not resolved, visit your subdivision office in person with your complaint number, your CNIC, a copy of the bill and your meter photo, and ask for the status in writing.

Never pay anyone who contacts you about a complaint. FESCO does not ask for payment to register or resolve a complaint, and no official will ask you to send money by mobile wallet or bank transfer to speed things up. Lodge complaints only through 118, the CCMS portal or your subdivision office.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track my FESCO complaint?

Track it on the CCMS portal at ccms.pitc.com.pk using your complaint or reference number, or call the FESCO helpline on 118 and give them the number. Keep the complaint number safe — without it, tracking and follow-up are much harder.

How long does a billing correction take?

It varies depending on the type of complaint and whether a meter re-reading or site visit is needed. Keep your complaint number and follow up regularly on the CCMS portal or by calling 118. If it is not resolved, visit your subdivision office in person with your complaint reference.

Can I complain without a reference number?

A complaint about a bill or a meter almost always needs your 14-digit reference number, because that is how FESCO identifies your connection. If you have lost it, find it on any old bill or ask at your subdivision office before complaining.

Can I check FESCO bill by CNIC?

No. FESCO bills cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The official PITC portal only accepts a 14-digit Reference Number or a 10-digit Customer ID. Use the bill checker on our homepage to validate your number, then check it on the official portal.
