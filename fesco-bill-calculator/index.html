How the calculator works

Your FESCO bill is built from five parts, then GST is added on the subtotal:

  1. Energy charge — your units × the rate for your slab.
  2. Fixed charge — a monthly charge per kW of your sanctioned load (the load printed on your bill).
  3. FPA (fuel price adjustment) — a per-unit charge that changes every month with fuel costs.
  4. QTA (quarterly tariff adjustment) — a per-unit adjustment that changes every quarter.
  5. FC surcharge (financing cost surcharge) — Rs 3.23 per unit (Rs 1.43 for protected consumers; lifeline consumers are exempt).

Then GST at 18% is charged on the subtotal of all five. That is why the “effective rate per unit” on your bill is always much higher than the headline slab rate — for example, an unprotected consumer at 350 units pays Rs 36.46 per unit in energy charges but about Rs 49.74 per unit all-in.

The slab-crossing effect: FESCO's domestic tariff is a single-slab tariff. If you use 350 units, all 350 units are charged at the 301–400 slab rate (Rs 36.46) — not the first 100 at one rate and the rest at another. Using just a few units over a slab boundary (for example 201 instead of 200) raises the rate on your entire consumption, which can add hundreds of rupees at once. Staying under a boundary is the single biggest way to cut your bill.

Why your actual bill may differ

  • Sanctioned load: the calculator defaults to 1 kW. If your sanctioned load is 2 or 3 kW, your fixed charge doubles or triples.
  • FPA and QTA of your billing month: both change regularly. Copy the exact figures from your own bill for the closest estimate.
  • Arrears and late payment surcharge: unpaid amounts from earlier bills carry forward with a surcharge — the calculator does not include them.
  • Meter reading errors: if the reading on your bill is higher than your actual meter, your bill is wrong. Compare the readings and file a complaint if they do not match.

Frequently asked questions

How is a FESCO bill calculated?

Energy charge (units × slab rate) + fixed charge (per kW of sanctioned load) + FPA + QTA + financing cost surcharge, then 18% GST on the subtotal. Unprotected consumers pay one slab rate on all units.

What is the FESCO unit rate in November 2026?

Lifeline Rs 3.95 (1–50) and Rs 7.74 (51–100); protected Rs 10.54 (1–100) and Rs 13.01 (101–200); unprotected Rs 22.44 to Rs 47.20 depending on slab; TOU peak Rs 46.85, off-peak Rs 34.53.

Why is my FESCO bill higher than the calculator shows?

Usually a higher sanctioned load, a different FPA for your billing month, arrears or late-payment surcharge, or a slab crossing. Compare each line with your actual bill using our guide to reading your FESCO bill.

Can I check FESCO bill by CNIC?

No. FESCO bills cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The official PITC portal only accepts a 14-digit Reference Number or a 10-digit Customer ID. Use the bill checker on our homepage to validate your number, then check it on the official portal.

What is FPA on a FESCO bill?

FPA is the fuel price adjustment — a per-unit charge that changes every month with fuel costs. The FPA for August 2026 consumption was +Rs 1.1086 per unit. The calculator defaults to that latest verified rate; change it to the FPA printed on your own bill for a closer estimate.

Is this calculator the same as my official bill?

No — it is an estimate using the notified tariff. Your official bill is issued by FESCO through the PITC portal and may differ because of your exact sanctioned load, the FPA and QTA of your billing month, arrears or adjustments. Always confirm the final amount on your official bill before paying.
