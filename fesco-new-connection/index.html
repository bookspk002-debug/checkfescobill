FESCO New Connection — Apply Online (November 2026)

Building a new house or opening a shop with no electricity connection? You can apply for a new FESCO connection online through the ENC portal and track the application yourself at each stage.

Updated: November 2026

Where to apply: the ENC portal

New connection applications are made through the ENC (Electricity New Connection) portal at fesco.enc.com.pk — the official online system for new connections in FESCO's eight districts: Faisalabad, Sargodha, Jhang, Toba Tek Singh, Chiniot, Khushab, Mianwali and Bhakkar.

Do not confuse the ENC portal with the bill portal. The bill portal at bill.pitc.com.pk/fescobill is for checking bills for an existing connection, using a 14-digit reference number. The official FESCO website is fesco.com.pk. This website is not affiliated with FESCO or PITC, and we do not process applications.

Documents you usually need

Have your documents ready before you start the online form. The exact list depends on your application type, and the ENC portal shows the requirements — but you will usually need:

  • A copy of your CNIC — the applicant's Computerised National Identity Card.
  • Proof of property ownership or tenancy — for example, a property document if you own the premises, or a rent or tenancy agreement if you are a tenant applying for the connection.
  • Load details — the load you are applying for, in kilowatts, based on the appliances and equipment you plan to run. This is the sanctioned load that will be printed on your bills later, and it affects your fixed charge, so think it through before you apply.

Scan or photograph your documents clearly before you upload them — a blurry copy is a common reason for delays. Keep the originals safe, as you may be asked to show them later.

The process, step by step

  1. Apply online. Fill in the application on the ENC portal at fesco.enc.com.pk, upload your documents, and submit it. Note the application or tracking number you are given — you will need it to track the application.
  2. Survey and feasibility. FESCO carries out a survey of your premises to check whether a connection is feasible — for example, whether the nearest pole or transformer can serve the load you applied for, and what work is needed to connect you.
  3. Demand notice issued. If the connection is feasible, FESCO issues a demand notice. This is the official document that states the amount you must pay for the connection, including the cost of the meter and any connection work.
  4. Pay the demand notice. Pay the amount shown on the demand notice by its due date. Keep the payment receipt — it is your proof that the connection charges have been paid.
  5. Meter installation. After payment, FESCO installs the meter at your premises and energises the connection. Once the meter is installed, you have a reference number, and your first bill will be issued in the next billing cycle. From then on, you can check your bill online with that reference number.

How much does a new connection cost?

The fee for a new connection depends on the sanctioned load you apply for and on the work needed to connect your premises, and the exact amount is shown on the demand notice issued for your application. There is no single fixed fee that applies to every connection, so do not rely on a figure quoted by someone else for a different property or load. Any figure you see quoted online should be treated as approximate only and confirmed on the ENC portal — the demand notice is the only official statement of what you owe. If anyone asks you to pay a connection fee before a demand notice has been issued, be cautious and check with FESCO first, or file a complaint through the CCMS portal at ccms.pitc.com.pk, or call the helpline on 118.

Do not pay before the demand notice. The demand notice is the official document that states your connection charges. Pay only the amount on that notice, and keep the receipt.

Domestic and commercial connections

Most applications are for one of two load types. A domestic connection is for a home, billed on the domestic tariff under NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026, with lifeline, protected and unprotected rates depending on usage. A commercial connection is for a shop, office or other business premises, billed on the commercial tariff.

Apply for the right type. A shop running on a domestic connection, or a home registered as commercial, will be billed wrongly — if you are unsure, ask at your subdivision office before you apply. Your sanctioned load also matters afterwards: the fixed charge on your monthly bill is charged per kilowatt of sanctioned load. Estimate your bills with our bill calculator and check the current unit rates before you decide on the load.

Tracking your application

Track your application on the ENC portal at fesco.enc.com.pk, using the application or tracking number given when you applied. The portal shows the current stage — waiting for a survey, demand notice issued, or meter installed. Check regularly and act promptly when a stage changes, especially when a demand notice is issued, because it has a due date. If the status has not changed for a long time, call the FESCO helpline on 118 or visit your subdivision office with your application number and CNIC.

Load increase for an existing connection

If you already have a FESCO connection and need more load — for example, you have added an air conditioner or a water pump — you apply in the same way, through the ENC portal. This is a load increase on an existing connection: apply online, a survey is carried out, a demand notice is issued for the additional load, and you pay it before the higher load is sanctioned. Do not simply run equipment beyond your sanctioned load — your meter and wiring are rated for the load on your bill, and overloading can damage them.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a new connection take?

It varies by area and workload, and the demand notice stage is usually the longest. There is no fixed number of days — track your application on the ENC portal and follow up with FESCO if it stalls.

How do I track my application?

Track it on the ENC portal at fesco.enc.com.pk, using the application or tracking number given when you applied. If the portal shows no update, call the FESCO helpline on 118 or visit your subdivision office.

What documents do I need?

Usually a copy of your CNIC, proof of property ownership or tenancy, and details of the load you are applying for. The ENC portal lists the exact documents required for your application type.

Can I apply without visiting an office?

Yes — you can submit the application online through the ENC portal at fesco.enc.com.pk. A survey of your premises is still carried out by FESCO, and you may need to visit an office later, for example to pay the demand notice or to follow up.
