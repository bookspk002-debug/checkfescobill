FESCO Previous Bill Check — November 2026

Your current bill is only half the story. The official PITC portal also shows your last 12 months of FESCO bills — and that history is the best tool you have for spotting a wrong reading, understanding why a bill jumped, and checking whether you qualify as a protected consumer.

Updated: November 2026

How to see your 12-month bill history

The history is on the same official portal as your current bill: bill.pitc.com.pk/fescobill. There is no login and no fee. You need your 14-digit reference number, printed at the top-left of any old bill, or your 10-digit customer ID.

  1. Open the bill checker on the homepage. Go to the bill checker and type your 14-digit reference number (or 10-digit customer ID).
  2. Click “Open General Bill”. Your bill opens directly on the official PITC portal — no need to type the number again, and no captcha.
  3. Open the bill history. Alongside your latest bill, the portal shows the last 12 months of bills. Select the month you want to look at.
  4. View or download that month's bill. The bill for the selected month opens on screen. Print it, or save it as a PDF using your browser's print option.

Each month in the history is a full bill, not just a summary — you can see the units, the amount, the due date and the charges for that month. The steps are the same as for a duplicate bill, except you pick an earlier month from the history instead of the latest one.

Only 12 months are shown. The portal keeps the last 12 months of bills. If you need a bill older than that, the portal will not have it — ask at your subdivision office, taking your CNIC and meter details with you.

How to compare months and spot a sudden jump

A bill that suddenly doubles is alarming, but the history usually explains it. Compare the same three figures across months: units consumed, amount due, and the slab or category shown on the bill.

Start with units. If last month was 180 units and this month is 420, the amount will be far higher — and not just because of the extra units. FESCO's domestic tariff is a single-slab tariff: unprotected consumers pay one slab rate on all units, so crossing into a higher slab raises the rate on the whole bill. That is why a jump from 200 to 201 units can cost far more than a jump from 150 to 151. Our bill calculator shows this effect, and the unit rate page lists every slab under the current tariff, NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026.

Next, look for arrears. If a previous month was paid late or not in full, the unpaid amount carries forward. If the amount due jumped but the units stayed about the same, arrears or a late payment surcharge is the likely cause.

Also compare the meter readings printed on each bill. If the reading on one month's bill is far higher than the reading on your actual meter, it may have been recorded wrongly — take photos of your meter and file a complaint with FESCO. Save the PDFs of the months in question first; the history is your evidence. Seasonal patterns matter too: comparing this November with last November is more useful than comparing November with July, when fans and air conditioners run more.

Use the history to check your protected status

Protected status is one of the biggest factors in your bill: protected consumers pay Rs 10.54 per unit for 1–100 units and Rs 13.01 for 101–200 units, against Rs 22.44 and above for unprotected consumers. You are protected if you used 200 units or less in each of the last 6 consecutive months.

That rule is exactly why the 12-month history is so useful. Count back six months from your latest bill: if every month is 200 units or less, you should be on the protected rate. If even one month is over 200, you lose protected status — and your rate roughly doubles until you have six months at 200 units or less again. If the history shows six months at 200 or less but your latest bill still charges the unprotected rate, raise it with FESCO, taking those six bills as proof, or call the helpline on 118.

A quick check: open the history, write down the units for the last six months, and check whether any month is over 200. That one check tells you whether you are protected, and whether a high bill is explained by lost protected status.

What SMS 8118 can and cannot do

Texting your reference number to 8118 is a handy shortcut for the latest bill — you get the current amount back by reply. But SMS only gives you the latest bill, not the history. You cannot get last month's bill, a six-month comparison, or a printable copy by SMS. For any of those, use the portal.

SMS is best used as a quick check before you pay. For anything that needs a record — a dispute, a protected-status check, or a copy for your files — the portal history is the right tool.

If a month is missing from the history

Occasionally a month is missing from the 12-month history. The most common reason is timing: the bill for that month may not have been uploaded yet, or the billing cycle had not finished when you checked. Wait a day or two and check again, and confirm the bill month and due date shown for the months that are there.

If the month is still missing after the billing month has passed, call the FESCO helpline on 118 or visit your subdivision office with your CNIC and meter details. Ask them to confirm whether a bill was issued for that month and when it will appear on the portal. Do not assume a missing month means no bill was due — the amount may still be payable, and it may appear later as arrears. Frequently asked questions

How many months of history can I see?

The official PITC portal shows the last 12 months of FESCO bills for your reference number, including the latest bill. Older bills are not shown on the portal.

Can I download an old bill as PDF?

Yes. Open the month you want in the 12-month history, then use your browser's print option and choose "Save as PDF" to download a copy.

Why is one month missing?

Usually the bill for that month has not been uploaded yet, or it was not issued. If the billing month has passed and it is still missing, call the FESCO helpline on 118 or visit your subdivision office with your CNIC and meter details.

Can I check a previous bill by CNIC?

No. A FESCO bill cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The portal only accepts a 14-digit reference number or a 10-digit customer ID.
