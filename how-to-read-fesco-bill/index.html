How to Read Your FESCO Bill — November 2026

Your FESCO bill looks crowded, but it is really just a handful of sections: who you are, what your meter read, how many units you used, and what each charge adds up to. Here is every line explained, then a worked example.

Updated: November 2026

Your identity and connection

The top of the bill identifies you and your connection. The reference number sits at the top-left — a 14-digit number, sometimes with a U for urban or R for rural. It never changes, and it is the number you use to check your bill on the official PITC portal at bill.pitc.com.pk/fescobill, to text your bill to 8118, and to file a complaint. Next to it is your customer ID, a 10-digit number that also works on the portal. The bill also shows your name and address, the billing month, your tariff or category (lifeline, protected, unprotected or TOU) and your meter number.

Meter readings and units consumed

The middle of the bill is about your meter. The previous reading is the meter reading at the end of last month; the present reading is the reading at the end of this month. Your units consumed are simply the present reading minus the previous reading. If the present reading on your bill is higher than the number showing on your physical meter, the bill is wrong — photograph the meter and ask for a re-reading.

Energy charges

The energy charge is your units multiplied by your slab rate. Under NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026, effective 12 February 2026, lifeline is Rs 3.95 (1–50 units) and Rs 7.74 (51–100); protected is Rs 10.54 (1–100) and Rs 13.01 (101–200); unprotected runs from Rs 22.44 (1–100) up to Rs 47.20 (700+ units); TOU peak is Rs 46.85 and off-peak Rs 34.53. Unprotected consumers pay one slab rate on all units, so crossing a slab boundary raises the rate on the whole bill. See the full table on the FESCO unit rate page.

Fixed charges

The fixed charge is a monthly charge per kW of your sanctioned load — the load printed on your bill, usually 1–5 kW for a home. Lifeline consumers pay no fixed charge. Protected consumers pay Rs 200 per kW (1–100 units) or Rs 300 per kW (101–200). Unprotected fixed charges run from Rs 275 to Rs 675 per kW depending on the slab, and TOU consumers pay Rs 675 per kW.

FPA, QTA and FC surcharge

Three more per-unit lines follow. FPA (fuel price adjustment) changes every month with fuel costs — for example, +Rs 1.1086 per unit for August 2026 consumption — and it is charged on every unit you used. QTA (quarterly tariff adjustment) is a similar per-unit adjustment that changes every quarter. FC surcharge (financing cost surcharge) is Rs 3.23 per unit for unprotected and TOU consumers, Rs 1.43 per unit for protected consumers, and zero for lifeline consumers.

GST, arrears and the amount payable

GST at 18% is charged on the subtotal of all the lines above. It is a government tax, not a FESCO charge. Arrears are amounts left unpaid from earlier bills; they carry forward and are added to this month's charges, usually with a late payment surcharge on top. Finally, the bill shows two totals: the amount payable within the due date and the higher amount payable after the due date.

Charges that should not be on your bill: the Rs 35 PTV fee and the 1.5% electricity duty were removed from bills in July 2025. If either line still appears on a current bill, raise it with FESCO.

Worked example — a 350-unit unprotected bill

Follow this example with your own bill. It uses an unprotected consumer with 350 units, a 1 kW sanctioned load, FPA +Rs 1.1086 per unit and QTA Rs 0. At 350 units the slab rate is Rs 36.46 per unit and the fixed charge is Rs 475 per kW.

The effective cost is about Rs 49.74 per unit all-in, far above the headline slab rate of Rs 36.46. Check the same maths for your own units with our FESCO bill calculator.

Quick checks before you pay

  • Readings: does the present reading on the bill match your physical meter? If not, photograph the meter and file a complaint.
  • Units: present reading minus previous reading should equal the units charged.
  • Category: does the tariff match your usage? If you used under 200 units for six months running but the bill says unprotected, ask FESCO why.
  • Old charges: the Rs 35 PTV fee and 1.5% electricity duty should not appear — they were removed in July 2025.
  • Arrears: if arrears appear, check whether they are from a bill you already paid.
  • Due date: pay by the due date — the amount after it includes a late payment surcharge.

Frequently asked questions

What is FPA on my bill?

FPA is the fuel price adjustment — a per-unit charge that changes every month with fuel costs. For example, the FPA for August 2026 consumption was +Rs 1.1086 per unit. It is shown as its own line on your bill, so you can see exactly how much it adds.

Why is GST charged?

GST at 18% is a government tax charged on the subtotal of your bill — energy charge, fixed charge, FPA, QTA and FC surcharge added together. It is not a FESCO charge, and it applies to every domestic bill.

What does "arrears" mean?

Arrears are amounts left unpaid from earlier bills. They carry forward onto your current bill and are added to this month's charges, usually with a late payment surcharge. Paying the full amount by the due date keeps new arrears from building up.

Can I check FESCO bill by CNIC?

No. FESCO bills cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The official PITC portal only accepts a 14-digit Reference Number or a 10-digit Customer ID. Use the bill checker on our homepage to validate your number, then check it on the official portal.
