FESCO Load Shedding Schedule — November 2026

Looking for today's load shedding timings in your area? Read this first: there is no single fixed public timetable that stays correct, because FESCO load shedding is decided feeder by feeder and changes regularly. Here is how to find your feeder's real, current schedule.

Updated: November 2026

Be honest about this page: no website — including this one — can show you live load shedding timings. Any site that prints a fixed timetable for your area is showing old or guessed timings. The only reliable sources for your feeder's current schedule are the FESCO helpline on 118 and your subdivision office.

What a feeder is — and why it decides your schedule

FESCO supplies electricity through feeders: the power lines that run from a grid station or substation out to a group of streets and neighbourhoods. Your home is connected to one specific feeder, and load shedding is switched on and off at the feeder level — the whole feeder goes dark together, and the whole feeder comes back together.

That is why load shedding is never a city-wide timetable. FESCO's eight districts contain hundreds of feeders, each with its own schedule. Two streets a few minutes apart can be on different feeders and lose power at completely different times.

How to find your feeder's current schedule

  1. Find your feeder code. Your feeder code is printed on your FESCO bill. Note it down once you find it — you will need it every time you ask about the schedule. If you cannot find it, your subdivision office can tell you which feeder supplies your area.
  2. Call the helpline — 118 (toll-free). Give the operator your feeder code, or your 14-digit reference number if you do not know it, and ask for the current schedule for your feeder. This is the quickest way to get today's timings.
  3. Or ask at your subdivision office. Your nearest FESCO subdivision office holds the current schedule for the feeders in its area. This is also the right place to ask if the timings you were given do not match what actually happens on the ground.

Because schedules change, ask again whenever the pattern of outages in your area changes.

Why schedules change

Load shedding schedules are not fixed for the year. They change for several reasons:

  • Line losses on the feeder. Feeders with higher losses — electricity lost to theft, faulty lines or unpaid bills in the area — generally face longer load shedding than feeders with lower losses. This is the main reason one neighbourhood can have far more outages than the next.
  • Total load. When overall demand rises, for example in the summer when fans and air conditioners run all day, more load shedding is needed to balance the system. In cooler months, when demand falls, schedules usually ease.
  • Season. Summer and winter put very different loads on the system, so schedules are revised as the seasons change.
  • Maintenance. Planned maintenance on a grid station, transformer or line can cause extra or shifted outages in the affected feeder's area. These are usually temporary and end once the work is done.

That is also why two areas that had the same schedule in winter can have very different schedules in summer, and why a schedule printed on a website in one month is unreliable the next.

Why does my area have more load shedding than the next street?

It almost always comes down to the feeder. Your street and the next street may look like one neighbourhood, but if they are fed by different feeders, they follow different schedules. The feeder with higher line losses gets longer outages. If you believe your area is being shed more than the announced schedule, report it on the helpline 118 or through a complaint on the CCMS portal, giving your feeder code and the times the power went off.

What to do during load shedding

Until the schedule for your feeder improves, a few practical habits make outages easier to live with:

  • Keep a UPS or inverter charged. It keeps fans, lights and phone charging going through an outage. Make sure its battery is in good condition before the summer season.
  • Charge phones and power banks while the power is on.
  • Plan heavy work around the schedule. Once you know your feeder's timings from the helpline, run the washing machine, water motor and ironing while the power is on, not across an outage window.
  • Save units where you can. Lower usage does not change your feeder's schedule, but it does lower your bill — and staying under 200 units a month keeps you in the protected category, which roughly halves your energy rate. See the FESCO unit rate page.
  • Protect appliances. Switch off sensitive electronics during an outage and wait a moment after the power returns before switching them back on.

Load shedding vs a fault — how to tell the difference

Not every outage is load shedding. If your power is off but your neighbours on the same street still have power, the problem is more likely a local fault — a tripped breaker, a blown fuse or a fault on your own line. If the whole street is dark at a time that matches your feeder's schedule, it is almost certainly load shedding. If the whole street is dark well outside the scheduled hours, report it on 118 so FESCO can check whether a fault has developed on the feeder.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find my feeder number?

Your feeder code is printed on your FESCO bill. If you cannot find it, call the FESCO helpline on 118 with your 14-digit reference number, or ask at your subdivision office — they can tell you which feeder supplies your area.

Why does my area have more load shedding than the next street?

Load shedding is decided feeder by feeder, not street by street. Two nearby streets can be on different feeders with different line losses, so one feeder may face longer outages than the other. Schedules also change with total load, season and maintenance.

Where can I see today's FESCO load shedding schedule?

There is no single fixed public timetable that stays correct. The reliable sources for today's schedule are the FESCO helpline on 118 and your subdivision office. No website, including this one, can show live timings.

Can I check FESCO bill by CNIC?

No. FESCO bills cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The official PITC portal only accepts a 14-digit Reference Number or a 10-digit Customer ID. Use the bill checker on our homepage to validate your number, then check it on the official portal.
