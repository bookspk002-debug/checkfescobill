FESCO Bill — Chiniot (November 2026)

Chiniot is one of FESCO's eight districts, a compact district on the Chenab river, known for its furniture workshops as well as its town households and surrounding villages. The bill check is the same as everywhere in the FESCO region — one portal, one reference number — but a workshop and a home on the same street can be billed very differently.

Updated: November 2026

How to check your FESCO bill in Chiniot

All you need is the 14-digit reference number from any old bill — the only key the portal accepts, not your CNIC, name or meter number.

  1. Find your reference number — and its U or R. It is at the top-left of any previous bill: 14 digits, marked U (urban) or R (rural). Most village connections are R; town connections are usually U.
  2. Use our homepage checker. Enter your number in the checker at the top of our homepage — it is the fastest way to reach the official portal.
  3. Click Open General Bill. Your bill opens directly on the official PITC portal in a new tab — no need to type the number again, and no captcha.
  4. Read, print or download. The portal shows your latest bill with the amount, due date and units used, plus your last 12 months of billing history.

Chiniot: town households, furniture workshops and villages

Chiniot sits inside FESCO's eight-district region — Faisalabad, Sargodha, Jhang, Toba Tek Singh, Chiniot, Khushab, Mianwali and Bhakkar — which together serve roughly 4 to 5.4 million consumers. FESCO's headquarters is in Faisalabad, and Chiniot is served by the same network, the same portal, the same helpline 118 and the same subdivision offices as the rest of the region. What is different here is the mix: town households, furniture and woodwork workshops, small shops, and villages around the town.

For workshops, the two lines on the bill that matter most are the tariff category and the sanctioned load. Industrial and commercial connections are billed on different tariffs from domestic ones, and the fixed charge is calculated per kW of sanctioned load — so a workshop connection costs more every month even before a single unit is used. If the category on your bill does not match how the connection is actually used, raise it with FESCO rather than simply paying a bill that looks too high.

For households, the more common surprise is the slab. Domestic consumers are "protected" — paying the lower protected rates — only if they used 200 units or less in each of the last six consecutive months. One month over 200 units loses protected status and roughly doubles the energy rate, so watch usage in the hot months.

No internet? Use SMS. Text your reference number to 8118 and your bill details come back by SMS — no internet and no browser needed. Helpline 118 is the backup.

November 2026 rates — what Chiniot consumers pay

The current domestic tariff is NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026, effective 12 February 2026: lifeline Rs 3.95 (1–50) and Rs 7.74 (51–100); protected Rs 10.54 (1–100) and Rs 13.01 (101–200); unprotected Rs 22.44 (1–100) to Rs 47.20 (700+). On top come the FC surcharge (Rs 3.23/unit; Rs 1.43 protected), monthly FPA, quarterly adjustment and 18% GST. The Rs 35 PTV fee and 1.5% duty were removed in July 2025.

One rule deserves attention in a rural district: domestic consumers keep the lower protected rates only if they used 200 units or less in each of the last six consecutive months. A single month over 200 units ends protected status and roughly doubles the energy rate. To estimate a domestic bill, use our FESCO bill calculator with your units and sanctioned load; agricultural connections are billed on their own tariff.

Practical tips for Chiniot consumers

  • Check the tariff category on your bill. A workshop billed as domestic — or a home billed as commercial — will be wrong every month until the category is fixed.
  • Keep the reference number safe. Without it you cannot check the bill online at all — the portal does not accept CNIC, name, address or meter number. Save it in your phone and keep one old bill filed away.
  • Estimate first, then verify. Use our FESCO bill calculator to estimate a domestic bill from your units, then compare it with the official figure. A big gap usually means a different sanctioned load, arrears, or a slab crossing.
  • Check before the due date. Bills carry a late-payment surcharge once the due date passes, so check your bill as soon as it is issued.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my FESCO bill in Chiniot?

Enter your 14-digit reference number (or 10-digit Customer ID), including the U or R shown on your bill, in the checker on our homepage and click Open General Bill — your bill opens directly on the official PITC portal, with no re-typing and no captcha. Your latest bill appears with the amount and due date. You can also text your reference number to 8118 or call helpline 118.

What does U/R mean on my bill?

U stands for urban and R stands for rural. The letter is part of your reference number and tells the portal whether your connection is in a town or a village. Choose the same U or R on the portal's selector as printed on your bill — the wrong one can stop the bill from loading. Most village connections in Chiniot are R; town connections are usually U.

Can I check FESCO bill by CNIC?

No, only a 14-digit Reference Number or 10-digit Customer ID works. FESCO bills cannot be checked by CNIC number, name, address or meter number. The number is printed at the top-left of any old bill.
